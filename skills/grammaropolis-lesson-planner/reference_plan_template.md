# Plan template

Plans follow this fixed section order so they read the same everywhere a Grammaropolis plan appears.

**Why the structure is mechanical, not prose-only.** Named, ordered H2 headings (Hook, Learn, Assess, Practice, Create, Certified, Materials, Standards) so each section can be found by name. Character flavor and classroom detail live inside each section's body text, never in the heading itself. A `**Time:**` line opens every cycle-step section with a minutes range, taken from the timing split in `reference_teaching_cycle.md`, so the minutes sum exactly to the requested period length.

## The skeleton

```
# Lesson Plan: {Topic}, Grade {grade}

**Standard(s):** {the code(s), written short (L.1.1.B), each quoted verbatim once, from reference_standards_extract.md. When the teacher gave a code, from a pacing guide or their district, that code comes first, written as they gave it.}
**Total time:** {N} minutes
**Materials:** {short list; only what the classroom already has plus what this plan prints}

## Hook
**Time:** {from the timing split: 2 min}
{A character line or a one-sentence framing that makes the concept impossible to ignore.}

## Learn
**Time:** {from the timing split: 9 min at 30, 12 at 45}
{Direct instruction: 2-3 worked examples, rule stated after the examples, not before, and at least one line where the lead character states the rule through who they are.}

## Assess
**Time:** {from the timing split: 3 min at 30, 5 at 45}
{ONE exit-ticket-style question set (3-5 questions), shown on the board as well as read aloud, scored informally, not for a grade.}

## Practice
**Time:** {from the timing split: 7 min at 30, 12 at 45}
{One low-stakes activity: a sort, a match, a cloze, or (Grades 1-2) a physical/interactive task.}

## Create
**Time:** {from the timing split: 7 min at 30, 12 at 45}
{A short prompt for original student writing or speech, plus 1-2 sentence starters for grades that need them.}

## Certified
**Time:** {from the timing split: 2 min}
{One line from the Mayor naming what the student can now do. Optionally, ONE light pointer to the free lesson or the department's page, per the closing-pointer rule, never anywhere else in the plan.}

## Materials
{The full materials list, matching every item actually used above. Everything the lesson depends on is either something the classroom already owns or a page this plan prints.}

## Standards
{The full standard(s) cited, verbatim, with the code. If the lesson also touches a prerequisite or connects forward to a later grade's standard, name that by code plus a short gist, never by re-pasting the full text again.}
```

## Worked example: Grade 2, proper nouns, Nelson the Noun

Chosen as the example because it's free at every grade.

```
# Lesson Plan: Proper Nouns, Grade 2

**Standard(s):** L.1.1.B (use common, proper, and possessive nouns). Common/proper nouns is CCSS's grade-1 anchor standard for this exact skill; a grade-2 class working on it is reinforcing a standard introduced the year before, which is normal and worth saying to the teacher plainly rather than forcing a grade-2-only code that doesn't exist for this specific distinction.
**Total time:** 30 minutes
**Materials:** whiteboard or chart paper; 10 word cards per pair; students' own writing notebooks

## Hook
**Time:** 2 min
Nelson the Noun runs the tightest office in town, and every name that comes across his desk gets filed in one of two drawers. Ask the class: "If I said 'girl' and 'Maria,' which one names just one specific person?"

## Learn
**Time:** 9 min
Model 3 pairs on the board: girl/Maria, city/Boston, holiday/Thanksgiving. For each pair, point out that the specific name gets a capital letter and the general word doesn't. State the rule after the examples: a common noun names a person, place, or thing in general; a proper noun names one specific person, place, or thing, and always starts with a capital letter. Nelson's line to close Learn: "A common noun could be anybody's. A proper noun is somebody's name, and every name I file gets a capital letter."

## Assess
**Time:** 3 min
Write 4 words on the board and read each aloud (school, Boston, teacher, Mrs. Alvarez); students give a thumbs-up for proper, thumbs-down for common. Watch for students who can say why a word names one specific thing, not only that it has a capital letter.

## Practice
**Time:** 7 min
Partner sort: give each pair 10 word cards (mix of common and proper nouns from the classroom, e.g. "student," a made-up first name, "state," "Texas"). Students sort into two piles and check each other's work.

## Create
**Time:** 7 min
Prompt: "Write two sentences about someone or something specific in your own life, one common noun and one proper noun, each underlined." Sentence starter for students who need one: "My favorite ___ is ___."

## Certified
**Time:** 2 min
The Mayor: "By order of this town, you can now tell a common noun from a proper one, and you know why a name gets its capital letter." If some of the class needs another pass, the free lesson at https://grammaropolis.com/learn/pos/nelson/g2 reteaches this.

## Materials
Whiteboard or chart paper for the modeled pairs; 10 word cards per pair for the sort (can be handwritten); students' own notebooks for the Create step.

## Standards
L.1.1.B: "Use common, proper, and possessive nouns." Introduced at grade 1; this lesson reinforces it for a grade-2 class. Grade 2's own Nelson standards (L.2.1.B, L.2.2.C) cover irregular plural nouns and possessive nouns, a natural next lesson once this one lands.
```
