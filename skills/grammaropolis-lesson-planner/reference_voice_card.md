# Voice in a lesson plan

Who the characters are lives in `reference_cast_card.md`, the cast card every Grammaropolis teacher skill shares. This file says how much character a lesson plan carries and where. If a lesson needs more character detail than the cast card gives, keep the lesson plain rather than inventing.

## Where the character appears

- **The lead character** is the one who owns the lesson's topic on the cast card. The Mayor leads Sentence Factory and Writing Company lessons and capitalization, and he always gives the Certified line.
- **Hook:** one or two lines in the lead character's voice.
- **Learn:** at least one line where the lead character states the rule through who they are (Lucy's equals sign, Roger needing his noun). The rest of Learn is plain teacher language.
- **Certified:** one line from the Mayor.
- **Everywhere else** (Assess, Practice, Create, Materials, Standards) is plain teacher language, colleague to colleague.
- **At Grades 6-8**, offer a plain version with the character lines removed; the strip test below means it still teaches completely.

The plan passes both cast card tests: it teaches correctly with every character line removed, and each character line could only have come from that character.

## Example hook lines

Write new ones in the same register rather than reusing these word for word.

- **Nelson:** "Every person, place, thing, and idea in this town crosses my desk. Today we're opening the drawer marked proper nouns, and every one of them gets a capital letter."
- **Jake:** "Could we be more specific? We could. Watch what one describing word does to a plain noun."
- **Benny:** "You already know how to run. Today I'm coaching you on how to run carefully, quickly, and well, because an adverb tells how."
- **The Mayor:** "Every sentence in this town has a subject and a predicate. No exceptions. Let's see yours."

## Mechanical rules (every generated plan)

- **Zero em dashes, zero en dashes.** Use a comma, period, colon, semicolon, or parentheses instead, chosen by the sentence's grammatical role, never a substitute dash.
- **Oxford comma**, always, in every list of three or more.
- **Real grammatical terms only.** Say "the coordinating conjunction," "the direct object," "a manner adverb." Never invent a cute noun for a grammatical part (no "joiner," no "describer word" as a noun).
- **Canon terms** stay exactly as the cast card writes them.
