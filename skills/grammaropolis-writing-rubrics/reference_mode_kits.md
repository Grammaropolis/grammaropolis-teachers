# Reference: Mode Kits (per-mode categories, framing quotes, CCSS standards)

*One kit per writing mode. Each kit lists the CCSS standards family, the mode-specific category set (Grade 5 baseline; scale per `reference_grade_calibration.md`), the Mayor framing-quote template, the sourcing convention, and the Power Move. Category sets grew out of real middle-school classroom rubrics and the Grammaropolis writing framework.*

*Shared closing categories (all modes, always last, in this order): **Sentences** *(W.X.4, L.X.3)*, **Mechanics**, **Format**. Not repeated per kit.*

*Shared category names ("Title," "Opening Hook," "Topic Sentence," "Linking Words," "Conclusion") appear across kits, but never reuse tier descriptors across modes: each mode's version answers a different reader expectation. A persuasive hook stakes a position's urgency; a narrative hook drops the reader into a moment; an informative hook earns attention with a fact.*

---

## persuasive

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.1 (opinion at Grades 1-5, argument at 6-8). Sub-standards W.3.1.A-D through W.5.1.A-D; W.6.1.A-E through W.8.1.A-E. Power Move previews W.7.1.A.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Op-Ed Title *(W.5.1.A)*, Opening Hook *(W.5.1.A)*, Opinion Statement *(W.5.1.A)*, Reasons *(W.5.1.B)*, Facts and Details *(W.5.1.B)*, Linking Words *(W.5.1.C)*, Conclusion *(W.5.1.D)*, Power Move: Counterclaim *(optional at Grade 5; required Grade 6 and up; W.7.1.A)*.
- **Grades 6-8 renames and additions:** Opinion Statement becomes Claim Statement; Facts and Details becomes Evidence from Credible Sources; Linking Words becomes Cohesion Words, Phrases, and Clauses; ADD Formal Style *(W.6.1.D and up)*; ADD Sourcing Convention as its own category at Grade 7 and up. Counterclaim becomes required.
- **Framing quote (fixed, use verbatim at Grade 5):** *"A good persuasive paragraph is a small act of leadership. You take a position, you give your reasons, and you stand by them, with civility. The Mayor will accept nothing less."*
- **Sourcing convention:** informal citation at Grades 3-4 ("I read in a book that..."); simplified formal sourcing optional at Grade 5 ("according to Mrs. Alvarez, our librarian..."); the full convention (name + credential + introductory verb + direct quote) required at Grades 7-8, with real, credible sources per W.7.1.B.

## narrative

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.3. Sub-standards W.3.3.A-D; W.4.3.A-E through W.8.3.A-E.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Opening Hook *(W.5.3.A)*, Chronological Organization *(W.5.3.A, W.5.3.C)*, First-Person Past Tense *(W.5.3.B)*, Background Information *(W.5.3.A)*, Meaning: Thoughts and Feelings *(W.5.3.B, W.5.3.D)*, Characters *(W.5.3.B, W.5.3.D)*, Setting *(W.5.3.D)*, Dialogue and Punctuation *(W.5.3.B)*, Conclusion *(W.5.3.E)*, Power Move: Figurative Language *(optional at Grade 5; expected Grade 6 and up; L.5.5.A)*.
- **Kit notes:** chronological order with no unnecessary shifts; first-person past tense with no second person and no tense slippage; Background Information addresses the classic failure where the writer assumes the reader already knows their family and school; Meaning runs THROUGHOUT the piece, not as a summary at the end; dialogue instead of summary, punctuated correctly.
- **Framing template:** [a story as a gift to the reader] + [the core moves: order, voice, meaning] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"A story is the only place a reader gets to live a day of someone else's life. Tell it in order, tell it in your own voice, and tell the reader why it mattered. The Mayor expects to be moved."*
- **Sourcing convention:** none (personal narrative). Dialogue punctuation carries the mechanics weight.
- **Grade-band progression (matched to Grammaropolis's narrative lessons, all 8 grades):**
  - **G1-2 (a short story, sentence to short-paragraph scale):** Characters (a "who"), Something Happens (an event), Chronological Order (beginning-middle-end at G2, "and then" ordering at G1), Meaning: Feeling (how it felt), Conclusion (a real ending). Setting, First-Person Past Tense, Background Information, Dialogue, Title, and Figurative Language are NOT yet their own categories; the lessons introduce "a setting" only at Grade 3, so do not add Setting here. *(W.1.3; W.2.3.)*
  - **G3-4 (the story fills out):** ADD Title, Opening Hook *(W.3.3.A, W.4.3.A)*, Setting *(W.4.3.A; first appears in the Grade 3 lesson)*, Chronological Organization as its own category *(W.3.3.C, W.4.3.C)*, Dialogue and Description *(W.4.3.B; no dialogue-punctuation discipline yet at G3)*. Characters and Meaning carry up from G1-2. First-Person Past Tense emerges as a steady-narrator expectation (extrapolated; the lessons do not name point of view before Grade 6). Background Information stays simpler than G5. Figurative Language rare.
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** ADD Point of View *(W.7.3.A, W.8.3.A name point of view; the lessons introduce "a point of view" one grade earlier, at Grade 6)*. Meaning deepens to Internal Change (the Grade 6 lesson certifies "a mind that actually changed"). Conclusion becomes Reflective Conclusion at G7 *(W.7.3.E, W.8.3.E; the Grade 7 lesson wants an ending that "understands itself")*. ADD Narrative Voice Consistency at G8 (the Grade 8 lesson certifies "one honest person told it start to finish, never once broke character"). Figurative Language moves from optional to expected *(L.6.5.A)*. Dialogue and Punctuation full.

## descriptive

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.3.D (concrete words, sensory details) at Grades 4-8, with W.X.4 secondary. Grades 1-3 have no sensory-detail sub-standard (at Grade 3, W.3.3.D is a sense of closure, so never cite it here): cite L.1.6 or L.2.6 at Grades 1-2 (W.1.3 or W.2.3 when the description sits inside a story), and W.3.4, L.3.3.A, and L.3.6 at Grade 3. Grammaropolis teaches description at every grade, in its Write to Describe lessons for Grades 1-8.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Spatial Organization *(W.5.4)*, Object-as-Subject Construction *(W.5.4, L.5.3.A)*, Active Verbs *(L.5.3.A)*, Sensory Detail Variety *(W.5.3.D)*, Adjective Description *(W.5.3.D, L.5.6)*, Power Move: Personification *(optional at Grade 5; expected Grade 6 and up; L.5.5.A)*.
- **Kit notes:** spatial organization is the primary structural move (top-to-bottom, near-to-far; one method, no shifts); object-as-subject is THE craft move ("the tree leans against the fence," never "there is a tree"); no first-person action (the writer is invisible); active verbs in present tense with no tense slippage; adjectives woven in, never listed.
- **Framing template:** [description as making the reader see] + [pick an order, let the objects act, feed the senses] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"When you describe a place well, the reader stands in it. Choose your path through the scene, let the things themselves do the acting, and give the reader more than their eyes. The Mayor is watching. And listening. And, if you have done it right, smelling."*
- **Voice caution:** "prefer active verbs" is a drafting constraint for this mode, not a verdict on linking verbs in general. Frame active verbs as this mode's tool.
- **Grade-band progression (Grades 1-8; the craft steps below follow the Grammaropolis show-don't-tell progression):**
  - **G1-2 (a sentence or two of sensory detail, short-answer scale):** Sensory Detail (one or two senses; this progression's Show-Don't-Tell step is "draw the picture, do not name it" at G1 and "one sense detail replaces one feeling word" at G2), Describing Words (basic adjectives; *L.1.6, L.2.6*). NO Spatial Organization, NO Object-as-Subject, NO active-verb discipline (this progression does not introduce "active over passive" until G6), NO Title. Cite *L.1.6* or *L.2.6* (W.1.3 or W.2.3 inside a story); never a W.X.3.D code, which doesn't exist at these grades.
  - **G3-4 (organized description emerges):** ADD Spatial Organization (a single order, top-to-bottom or near-to-far), Sensory Detail Variety (more than one sense; this progression's G3 step is "two details replace a claim"), Active Verbs (this progression's G3 step is "action verbs by sense, ran to sprinted"; strong verbs, not yet the no-linking-verb discipline), Adjective Description woven not listed *(W.4.3.D; L.4.6)*. Object-as-Subject is not yet required (it arrives at the G5 baseline). *(Grade 3: W.3.4, L.3.3.A, L.3.6. Grade 4: W.4.3.D, W.4.4.)*
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** Personification moves from optional Power Move to EXPECTED *(L.6.5.A; this sequence treats personification as upper-grade craft)*. Active Verbs hardens to the no-linking-verb discipline (this progression's G6 step is "verb mood and voice, active over passive"; *L.8.3.A*). Object-as-Subject Construction mastery (the thing itself acts: "the tree leans against the fence," never "there is a tree"). Sensory restraint and subtext at G7-8 (this progression's "implication over statement" at G7, "restraint and subtext" at G8).

## expository-inform

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.2. Sub-standards W.3.2.A-D; W.4.2.A-E through W.5.2.A-E; W.6.2.A-F through W.8.2.A-F.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Opening Hook *(W.5.2.A)*, Topic Sentence *(W.5.2.A)*, Factual Information *(W.5.2.B)*, Source Citation *(W.5.2.B)*, Linking Words *(W.5.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.5.2.E)*.
- **Kit notes:** the To Inform discipline is facts only; opinions and appeals to emotion are off-mode (a piece that argues is Not Yet on mode grounds, however well it argues). Objectivity is the distinctive tier dimension: Gold information is factual, specific, and verifiable.
- **Framing template:** [informing as public service] + [promise the topic, deliver the facts, stay out of the way] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"Informative writing is a public trust. Tell the reader what is true, tell them how you know, and keep your opinions in your pocket. The Mayor has read the encyclopedia. Twice. He knows the difference."*
- **Grade-band progression (cross-checked against Grammaropolis's informative/explanatory lessons, divergences named):**
  - **G1-2 (a true fact or two, short-answer scale):** Topic (what the piece is about), True Facts (one or two factual sentences), Conclusion (a sense of closure). No Source Citation, no roadmap Topic Sentence, no Opening Hook. The tier dimension even here is objectivity: a fact, not a feeling. *(W.1.2, W.2.2, "supply some facts.")*
  - **G3-4 (the report fills out):** ADD Title, Topic Sentence *(W.3.2.A)*, Factual Information *(W.3.2.B, W.4.2.B)*, Linking Words *(W.3.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.3.2.D)*. Source Citation emerges as simplified "name where you learned it" at G4. DIVERGENCE from explain: inform pulls citation earlier (the informative/explanatory lessons withhold sourcing until Grade 7), because fact delivery is inform's whole discipline. Objectivity is the tier dimension throughout.
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** Source Citation becomes fuller (name plus where; *W.6.2.B* credible sources), full convention at G7-8 *(W.7.2.B)*. Linking Words becomes Cohesion Transitions *(W.6.2.C)*. ADD Formal Style *(W.6.2.E)*. Objectivity expectation rises (no opinion, no appeal to emotion). CCSS differentiation: inform cites *W.X.2.B* fact delivery; explain cites the *W.X.2* standard's "analysis" plus *W.X.2.D* "explain.".

## expository-explain

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.2 (same family as inform; the modes split on content discipline, not standard family).
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Opening Hook *(W.5.2.A)*, Topic Sentence *(W.5.2.A)*, How/Why Logic *(W.5.2.B)*, Examples and Details *(W.5.2.B)*, Linking Words *(W.5.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.5.2.E)*.
- **Kit notes:** How/Why Logic is the distinctive category: Gold means the steps or causes connect so the reader could re-explain it; Bronze means steps are present but out of order or with gaps. Explain answers how or why; inform delivers what.
- **Framing template:** [explaining as generosity] + [promise the question, walk the steps, land the answer] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"To explain something well is to hand the reader a working machine. Show them the parts, show them the order, and by the end they can run it without you. The Mayor once explained the entire municipal filing system this way. Voluntarily."*
- **Grade-band progression (matched to Grammaropolis's explanatory lessons, all 8 grades):**
  - **G1-2 (the two-stop tour, short-answer scale):** Topic/Destination (where we are going), Ordered Steps (two stops in order), What to Notice (one detail per stop), Conclusion (an ending that shows the way). No Title, no Opening Hook, no graded Linking Words (the linking-vocabulary ladder starts at G3). *(W.1.2; W.2.2.)*
  - **G3-4 (the explanation fills out):** ADD Title, Topic Sentence as roadmap *(W.3.2.A, W.4.2.A; the Grade 3 lesson asks for "a clear destination")*, How/Why Logic *(W.3.2.B, W.4.2.B; each step or reason connects to the next so the reader could re-explain it, not just a list in order)*, Examples and Details *(W.4.2.B)*, Linking Words *(W.3.2.C, W.4.2.C)*. Opening Hook simpler at G3.
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** ADD Source Citation at G7 (the Grade 7 lesson requires "every claim told me where it came from"; *W.7.2.B*). How/Why Logic deepens to Analysis (Why, Not Just What) at G8 (the Grade 8 lesson wants "the why at every one, not just the what"; the *W.8.2* standard's "analysis of relevant content"). Linking Words becomes Cohesion Transitions *(W.6.2.C, W.7.2.C)*. ADD Formal Style *(W.6.2.E)*.

## compare-contrast

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.2 (comparison as an informative/explanatory structure). At Grades 7-8 the Thesis Statement category enters.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Main Idea *(W.5.2.A)*, Block vs. Point-by-Point Organization *(W.5.2.A)*, Relevant Features *(W.5.2.B)*, Parallel Treatment *(W.5.2.A)*, Linking Words *(W.5.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.5.2.E)*, Power Move: Venn Diagram Reference *(optional)*.
- **Grades 7-8 additions:** Thesis Statement as its own category, distinct from Main Idea (a paragraph can have a clear main idea and a poor thesis; they are independent dimensions); the objectivity expectation rises.
- **Kit notes:** pick ONE organizational method (block: all of A, then all of B; point-by-point: feature by feature) and stay in it; Parallel Treatment is the discipline (discuss both subjects' features in the same order). Those two moves together are what make compare-contrast a mode rather than expository-about-two-things.
- **Framing template:** [comparison as fair judgment] + [choose a method, treat both sides in the same order, say what the comparison reveals] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"To compare two things fairly, you must give them the same treatment in the same order. Choose your method, hold to it, and let the reader see both clearly. The Mayor insists on nothing less than fairness."*
- **Grade-band progression (grounded in classroom practice; standards silent on the comparison structure below G6):**
  - **G1-2 (one likeness and one difference, short-answer scale):** Two Things Named (the pair), One Way Alike, One Way Different. Sized to a sentence or two; no paragraph-structure categories (no Main Idea, no organization method, no Title). Standards note: CCSS has no writing-strand comparison code below G6, so this band follows common classroom practice (a Grade 2 "alike and different" task) plus the general informative frame *(W.1.2, W.2.2)*, not to a comparison-specific standard.
  - **G3-4 (the paragraph emerges):** ADD Title, Main Idea / Topic Sentence (the roadmap; *W.3.2.A, W.4.2.A*), Relevant Features (the same features for both subjects; *W.4.2.B*), Block vs. Point-by-Point Organization in simplest form (pick one order and hold it), Parallel Treatment ("same order for both"), Linking Words *(W.3.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.3.2.D)*. Standards note: comparison as an organizing pattern still has no CCSS writing code until G6; comparison as a text structure is introduced in Grade 3 reading standards, used here as the arrival point.
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** the comparison structure now has a citable CCSS home at *W.6.2.A, W.7.2.A* ("strategies such as ... comparison/contrast"; at Grade 8 the strategy list folds into *W.8.2.A*'s "broader categories"); cite it with the comparison/contrast strategy phrase to differentiate from cause-effect (same sub-standard) and from explain. Plus the G7-8 additions above (Thesis Statement as its own category distinct from Main Idea; objectivity rises). ADD Cohesion Transitions *(W.6.2.C)*, Formal Style *(W.6.2.E)*.

## cause-effect

- **CCSS standards family:** W.X.2. Evidence and sourcing carry this kit's distinctive weight.
- **Categories (Grade 5 baseline):** Title, Topic Sentence *(W.5.2.A)*, Cause/Effect Pairs *(W.5.2.B)*, Evidence *(W.5.2.B)*, Logical or Chronological Organization *(W.5.2.A)*, Linking Words *(W.5.2.C)*, Conclusion *(W.5.2.E)*.
- **Kit notes:** the topic sentence contains the situation AND introduces the causes or effects (the roadmap demand); Gold cause/effect pairs correlate directly with the situation, and Not Yet pairs have little to do with it or strain belief; organization is by importance or by chronology, one method, no shifts; the conclusion introduces nothing new.
- **Sourcing convention:** name + credential + introductory verb + direct quote ("A school nutritionist states, ..."). Simplified at Grade 5 (name the source, skip credentials); the full convention at Grades 7-8. Introductory phrases: "According to," "For example."
- **Framing template:** [cause and effect as detective work] + [name the situation, trace the chain, prove each link] + [Mayor closer]. Example register: *"Every effect has a cause, and the writer's job is to show the chain link by link. Name what happened, explain why, and bring evidence. The Mayor accepts no coincidences."*
- **Grade-band progression (grounded in classroom practice; standards silent on the cause/effect structure below G6):**
  - **G1-2 (what happened and why, short-answer scale):** Something That Happened (the situation), and Why It Happened (one cause) or What Followed (one effect). Sized to a sentence or two; no topic-sentence roadmap, no evidence or sourcing, no Title. Standards note: CCSS has no writing-strand cause/effect code below G6, so this band follows common classroom practice (a Grade 2 "it happened because" task) plus the general informative frame *(W.1.2, W.2.2)*.
  - **G3-4 (the paragraph emerges):** ADD Title, Topic Sentence as roadmap (the situation plus a preview of causes or effects; *W.3.2.A, W.4.2.A*), Cause/Effect Pairs (each pair tied to the situation; *W.4.2.B*), Logical or Chronological Organization (one method), Linking Words *(W.3.2.C, "because, so")*, Conclusion *(W.3.2.D)*. Evidence introduced as "say how you know," not yet sourced. Standards note: cause/effect text structure is introduced in Grade 3 reading standards; the writing-strand code arrives at G6.
  - **G6-8 renames and additions:** the cause/effect structure gains its citable CCSS home at *W.6.2.A, W.7.2.A* ("strategies such as ... cause/effect"; at Grade 8 the strategy list folds into *W.8.2.A*'s "broader categories"); cite with the cause/effect strategy phrase to differentiate from compare-contrast (same sub-standard, different strategy word) and from explain. Evidence becomes sourced Evidence (name + credential + introductory verb + direct quote; simplified at G6, full at G7-8). ADD Cohesion Transitions *(W.6.2.C)*, Formal Style *(W.6.2.E)*.

## Linking-words vocabulary ladder (all modes)

Grade 3: "because, therefore, since, for example." Grade 4: "for instance, in order to, in addition." Grade 5: "consequently, specifically, for instance, in addition." Grades 6-8: cohesion phrases and clauses in formal register.
