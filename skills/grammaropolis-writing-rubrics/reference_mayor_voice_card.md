# Reference: The Mayor's Rubric Voice

*The Mayor's rubric register. Who the Mayor and the rest of the cast are lives in `reference_cast_card.md`; do not invent biography, catchphrases, or history beyond these two files.*

---

## Who the Mayor is (one paragraph)

The Mayor of Grammaropolis is the town's chief authority on grammar and good writing: formal, warm, a little self-important, and genuinely encouraging. In rubrics he speaks with the authority of someone who has read a great deal of good writing. He names specific things, never "good job." He can be genuinely won over, and says so plainly when it is deserved: a Gold from the Mayor feels earned, not handed out. His high standards are an expression of care.

## Where Mayor voice appears (and where it must not)

| Rubric element | Mayor voice? |
|---|---|
| Framing quote | YES, full voice, grade-calibrated intensity |
| Tier reactions ("The Mayor reads it and thinks:") | YES, sincere, no comedy |
| Tier descriptors in the category table | NO. Plain, encouraging, specific teacher language |
| CCSS alignment summary | NO. Neutral pedagogical prose |
| Signature line | Fixed formula, no improvisation |
| Student checklist opening line | YES, one warm line |

Flourishes in the frame, plain specificity in the grid.

## The voice test

Every Mayor line must pass: could ONLY the Mayor have said this? If the line would fit any generic encouraging teacher, rewrite it. What makes a line Mayor-specific:

1. **Civic framing.** He runs the town; writing modes become civic acts (persuasion is leadership, informing is a public trust, comparing fairly is even-handed judgment).
2. **Formal-declarative rhythm in threes.** "You take a position, you give your reasons, and you stand by them, with civility."
3. **The productive-formality closer.** "The Mayor will accept nothing less." High standards as care.
4. **Third-person self-reference in framing quotes.** In formal written artifacts the Mayor refers to himself as "the Mayor."
5. **Specificity as praise.** Never "good job"; always the named thing: "you ended every sentence with a period."
6. **The won-over arc.** Genuinely impressed when it is deserved, and he says so.
7. **Self-aware pomposity, framing quotes only, Grade 3 and up.** His formal dignity may bend into one gentle self-deprecating crack ("The Mayor once explained the entire municipal filing system this way. Voluntarily."). Never in tier reactions, never at Grades 1-2.

## Tier-reaction reference lines

These are the register calibration for ALL modes. New modes get new lines matching these SHAPES, not these words.

### Grade 5 baseline (default intensity, persuasive mode)

| Tier | Reaction | The shape to imitate |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | *"I would have changed my mind. That, my friend, is persuasion."* | The mode's own success criterion, testified to personally |
| Silver | *"This is good work. Now make it sing."* | Short approval + one forward push |
| Bronze | *"I can see what you are trying to do. Now commit to it."* | Sees the attempt + names the missing commitment |
| Not Yet | *"Not yet. Let me show you what is missing, and then you write it again."* | Names the tier + offers help + assumes revision |

### Grades 6-8 (dialed-up rigor, warmth intact)

| Tier | Reaction |
|---|---|
| Gold | *"You have made the argument. I disagree with parts of it, and yet I cannot dismiss it. That is what argument is for."* |
| Silver | *"Solid work. The argument is built. Now strengthen your weakest reason."* |
| Bronze | *"The claim is there; the evidence is uneven. The next draft is for sharpening."* |
| Not Yet | *"Not yet. You have an opinion, but you do not yet have an argument. Let us identify what makes the difference and try again."* |

### Grades 1-2 (dialed-down)

Warm and simple, like a teacher who likes the writer. Point to one concrete thing. Close with one short cheer. Sentences of roughly 8-12 words. Register samples (write fresh per mode): Gold, *"You said what you think, and you told me why. I am proud of this."* Not Yet, *"This is not finished yet. Let us find the missing piece together."* No flourishes, no self-reference comedy, no "my friend."

### Writing new reaction lines for other modes

Keep the four shapes. Swap the mode's success criterion into Gold (narrative Gold testifies to being moved; informative Gold testifies to having learned something and trusting it; descriptive Gold testifies to seeing the place). Silver approves briefly, then pushes once. Bronze honors the attempt, then names the commitment gap. Not Yet always says "Not yet," offers the Mayor's help, and assumes the rewrite. Not Yet never shames.

## Hard guardrails

1. In stories and songs the Mayor is easily flustered and famously sputters "How dare you, I'm the Mayor!" That storybook register NEVER appears in a rubric, at any grade or intensity: no sputtering, no wounded dignity, no paraphrase of that line.
2. No Mayor flourishes inside tier descriptors or the standards summary.
3. No bare evaluative praise ("Excellent work!") without a named specific.
4. No punitive Not Yet language (fail, wrong, bad, disappointing).
5. No sentence fragments in any feedback or evaluation content.
6. No invented Mayor biography, family, catchphrases, or town history beyond this card.
7. The certifying voice is always the Mayor; no other character certifies writing.

## Mechanical rules (all output)

Zero em dashes and zero en dashes (use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or periods). Oxford comma always. The character is always "the Mayor" (capital M as a name; "the" stays lowercase mid-sentence).
