# Reference: Grade Calibration for Rubrics

*Per-grade calibration for tier-descriptor language, category naming, category counts, Mayor voice intensity, and Power Move presence. Grades 1-8, one grade per rubric.*

---

## The master calibration table

| Grade | Mode-specific categories (full version) | Descriptor sentence cap | Vocabulary ceiling in descriptors | Mayor voice intensity | Power Move | Naming register |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 10 words | everyday words only | dialed-down | omitted | question form or plain noun ("My Reason") |
| 2 | 4-5 | 12 words | everyday words, one academic-word reach | gentle default | omitted | plain noun ("What I Think," "Linking Words") |
| 3 | 6 | 14 words | everyday + academic words | default | omitted (most modes) | plain academic ("Reasons," "Topic Sentence") |
| 4 | 7 | 14 words | everyday + academic words | default | optional, rarely earned | plain academic |
| 5 | 8-12 | 18 words | academic words, occasional domain word | default | optional | full academic ("Opinion Statement") |
| 6 | 12 | 20 words | academic + domain words | dialed-up begins | REQUIRED where the mode defines one | formal academic ("Claim Statement," "Formal Style") |
| 7 | 13 | 22 words | academic + domain words | dialed-up | required + acknowledged | formal academic + sourcing convention |
| 8 | 13 | 22 words | domain words expected | dialed-up | required + distinguished (own claim vs. opposing) | formal academic |

Reading the table:

- **Descriptor sentence cap** is a per-sentence maximum inside tier descriptors, not an average. A Grade 1-2 descriptor is one or two short complete sentences; a Grade 7-8 descriptor may run two fuller sentences. Complete sentences at every grade; no fragments.
- **Vocabulary**: "academic words" are cross-domain school words (organize, support, specific); "domain words" are writing-craft terms (counterclaim, cohesion, credibility). A "reach" is one such word used where the surrounding sentence makes its meaning plain.
- **Category counts** are mode-specific categories in the full version; the closing rows (Sentences, Mechanics, Format) are additional at every grade. The default rubric caps at 8 rows in all, per `reference_rubric_format.md`. Format simplifies at Grade 4 and below to accommodate handwritten work.

## What changes, band by band

### Grades 1-2: presence checks, question forms, warm floor

- Categories collapse to the essentials (persuasive at Grades 1-2: Topic and Opinion, Reason, Closing, plus Linking Words at Grade 2 only). The rubric checks PRESENCE of the move, not quality gradations.
- Student-facing derivations use child-friendly question forms ("Did I say what I think?"); the formal category name lives in the teacher rubric.
- CCSS codes appear in the teacher rubric only, never on any student-facing page.
- Mayor voice: dialed-down. Warm, simple, no flourishes. Example Not Yet register: "The reader can't tell what you're writing about or what you think. Try again."
- Sample tier set (Topic and Opinion): Gold, "The reader knows what you're writing about and what you think. You said both." Silver, "You said what you think, but the reader has to guess what you're writing about." Bronze, "You named the topic, but you didn't say what you think." Not Yet, "The reader can't tell what you're writing about or what you think. Try again."

### Grades 3-4: the standard structure fills in

- The five-part shape arrives (introduce, state, organize, link, conclude). Categories reach 6-7.
- Facts and Details emerges as its own category at Grade 4 (rolled into Reasons at Grade 3).
- Sourcing is informal ("I read in a book that...", "My dad told me that..."); no credential convention.
- Linking words: Grade 3 "because, therefore, since, for example"; Grade 4 "for instance, in order to, in addition."
- Handwritten work is the norm; the Format category accommodates it.
- Mayor voice: default. Warm and specific; at most one gentle flourish in the framing quote.

### Grade 5: the baseline calibration

- The fullest single-paragraph rubric: 8-12 mode-specific categories in the full version, full academic naming, optional Power Move, simplified formal sourcing introduced as a stretch.
- Linking words: "consequently, specifically, for instance, in addition."
- Mayor voice: default; the reaction reference lines in `reference_mayor_voice_card.md` are calibrated here.

### Grades 6-8: the argument break

- The CCSS framing shifts from opinion to argument at Grade 6. This is a pedagogical break, not a rename: Claim replaces Opinion, Counterclaim becomes required, Formal Style enters as its own category, and (persuasive, Grade 7 and up) the full sourcing convention becomes required.
- Compare-contrast at Grades 7-8 adds Thesis Statement as a category distinct from Main Idea.
- Tier descriptors may carry two-clause sentences and domain vocabulary.
- Mayor voice: dialed-up. Full formal register; he treats Grade 6-8 writers as capable of real argument, so his reactions become more rigorous while staying warm underneath.

## The three portability invariants (hold at every grade)

1. **Same tier names.** Gold / Silver / Bronze / Not Yet, every grade, every mode.
2. **Same Mayor character.** Warm, specific, encouraging; intensity scales, identity does not.
3. **Categories scale by addition, not replacement.** A grade's categories are a superset of the previous grade's concepts; names may mature.

## Calibration cross-check (run before delivering)

1. Does any descriptor sentence exceed the grade's cap? Rewrite it at level.
2. Does any descriptor use vocabulary above the grade's ceiling without an immediate context clue? Replace or scaffold it.
3. Does the default rubric exceed 8 rows, or does a full version leave out a category the kit lists for the grade? Fix it.
4. Is the Mayor voice intensity right for the grade (dialed-down 1, gentle 2, default 3-5, dialed-up 6-8)?
5. Is the Power Move present or absent per the grade rule (omitted 1-3, optional 4-5, required 6 and up where the mode defines one)?
6. Do category names match the grade's naming register?
7. At Grades 6-8 persuasive: did Opinion become Claim, and is Formal Style present? At Grade 7 and up: is the sourcing convention its own category?
