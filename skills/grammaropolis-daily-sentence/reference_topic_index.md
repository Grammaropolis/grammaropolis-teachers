# Topic index

The map from a teacher's request ("commas," "fix-its on run-ons," "pronouns for grade 6") to a Grammaropolis department, the character who leads the warm-up, and the grades where Grammaropolis teaches the topic. Match on the grammar term first; teachers rarely say the character's name.

**Grades column.** These are the grades where the Grammaropolis catalog teaches the topic. A request outside that range isn't refused: say where Grammaropolis usually introduces it and offer a gentle version or a nearby focus (see SKILL.md, Step 1).

**Free topics.** Every department has at least one free topic on grammaropolis.com. The rows below that carry a page are the ones this skill points to, for the single closing pointer. Every other topic gets no link, or at most the plain mention of grammaropolis.com.

Interjections are taught at grades 1, 3, 6, and 8. At other grades, build the warm-up anyway at the requested grade's level, and cite the nearest listed grade's standard, saying so.

**Verbals** (gerunds, participles) are Vinny the Action Verb in costume, doing a noun's or an adjective's job. They are never Nelson or Jake. The infinitive has no single costume; the Mayor leads it.

**Why dashes are not built.** This skill's output never contains a dash character, so it cannot show students a correct dash. Offer one of two alternatives. Parentheses or commas around extra information teach the same decision a dash makes: whether to separate an aside from the main sentence. A hyphen week on compound modifiers (*a well-known song*) is also available, but it's a different lesson, since a hyphen joins words rather than separating ideas.

## Sentence Factory

The Mayor runs the Sentence Factory and leads every Sentence Factory warm-up. Officer Period may guest on run-on days; Connie may guest on combining days. Slang speaks only on a formal-or-informal day, where he supplies the informal line (see the voice card).

**Mixed review belongs to the Mayor.** Because he hosts the Sentence Factory and certifies the town's work, the Mayor leads every mixed-review day, whatever error types it mixes. A specialist (Chief Comma for a comma error, say) may guest for one line.

## The Writing Company (sentence-level craft only)

Warm-ups here are Notice and Build It days about word choice and sentence craft, never a full writing assignment. The Mayor hosts; a guest can make the point (Nelson for specific nouns, Vinny for strong verbs, Jake for vivid description).
