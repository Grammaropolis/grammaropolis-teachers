# Voice on an exit ticket

Who the characters are lives in `reference_cast_card.md`, the cast card every Grammaropolis teacher skill shares. This file says how much character an exit ticket carries. On an assessment, the character is a welcome at the door, never part of the test. If a check needs more character detail than the cast card gives, keep it plain rather than inventing.

## How much character an exit ticket gets

- **One framing line** at the top of the student page, spoken by the character who owns the topic on the cast card, in the first person ("Chief Comma here! ..."), not narration about them. Optional. The line points at the rule through who the character is.
- **One sign-off line** at the bottom of the student page, often from the Mayor, who certifies finished work. Optional.
- **Items are plain.** No character dialogue inside stems or options, no character names as the people in item sentences, and no item that requires knowing the cast. "Which word would Nelson file?" tests brand knowledge, not nouns; write "Which word is a noun?"
- **Drop the character** when the teacher asks for a plain version (a common request at grades 6-8), for a formal quiz the teacher will file, or when framing would crowd a grade 1-2 page.
- **The key has no character voice.** It speaks to the teacher, colleague to colleague: plain, specific, and brief.
- **A formal-or-informal check** may use Slang's register role from the cast card: his one informal line is the item's starting point, and the student decides who it fits.

## Register

Warm, playful, and confident without being loud. Encouraging about mistakes: a wrong answer is information, never failure. Never words like "fail," "wrong," or "bad" on the student page.

## Example framing and sign-off lines

Write new ones in the same register rather than reusing these.

- **Nelson:** "Every noun in town crosses my desk, and I file each one by name. Let's see how many you can file correctly."
- **Connie:** "Two ideas are waiting to be joined. Pick the FANBOYS word that shows how they connect."
- **Chief Comma:** "Commas follow orders, not breaths. Put each one where the rule says it goes."
- **The Mayor (sign-off):** "You finished the check and showed your work. The Mayor has noticed."

## Mechanical rules

- **Zero em dashes, zero en dashes**, anywhere. Use the punctuation the sentence's grammar calls for.
- **Oxford comma** in every list of three or more.
- **Real grammatical terms only.** "The coordinating conjunction," "the direct object," "a manner adverb." Never invent a cute noun for a grammatical part.
- **Canon terms** stay exactly as the cast card writes them, and appear at most once per check.
