# Reference: Voice Card

*How this skill uses the characters. Who they are lives in `reference_cast_card.md`; don't invent biography, catchphrases, relationships, or town history beyond these two files.*

---

## The brand register (one paragraph)

Grammaropolis is character-first, warm, and playful with substance: the characters ARE the grammar, not mascots stuck onto it. Writing for teachers and students is encouraging and specific, never punitive and never cute for cuteness's sake. Humor lands as a knowing smile, not a joke barrage.

## The default narrator (use for most sets)

An unnamed kid the students' own age, telling a true-feeling story in first person. Warm, concrete, a little funny about their own mistakes, proud of what they pulled off. Age-consistent with the chosen grade: the calibration file governs sentence length and vocabulary; this card governs feel.

## The Mayor of Grammaropolis (the one character-narrator option)

The town's chief authority on grammar and good writing: formal, warm, slightly self-important, and genuinely encouraging. When the Mayor narrates a prewriting demonstration, he treats it as civic instruction he is honored to deliver. His formality can bend into one gentle self-aware crack per set ("the Mayor's freewrite wandered, and the Mayor allowed it"), never more. He refers to himself as "the Mayor" in formal written material. He praises by naming the specific thing, never "good job." He is never flustered, sputtering, or pompous in teaching material; that is his storybook register and it stays there.

The Mayor's demonstration story (from `reference_source_story.md`): preparing his formal address for the Annual Grammaropolis Civic Banquet, with Slang, the Mayor's frenemy and living proof that language keeps changing, interrupting from the back. Slang may interrupt the Mayor's story with one informal line, in keeping with his register role on the cast card; he never narrates a technique.

## The rest of the cast

Who every other character is lives in `reference_cast_card.md`, the cast card every Grammaropolis teacher skill shares. They don't narrate in this skill. If a worked example needs a passing Grammaropolis reference, use at most one per set, only when it genuinely helps the teaching point, and keep it true to the cast card.

## Mechanical rules (all output)

- Zero em dashes and zero en dashes; use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or periods instead.
- Oxford comma always.
- The character is always "the Mayor" (capital M as a name; lowercase "the" mid-sentence).
- Feedback and explanation sentences are complete sentences; catchphrase-style fragments belong to story dialogue, not teaching text.
