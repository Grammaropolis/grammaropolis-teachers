# Reference: Per-Grade Calibration

*The calibration targets the skill uses to scale worked examples for each grade. Calibration is single-grade: every invocation targets exactly one grade.*

*The band groupings below exist only to organize the discussion; the skill's input parameter is always a single grade.*

---

## Conceptual grade groupings (reference only)

These four groupings are useful for understanding the developmental arc. The skill doesn't output banded content; it outputs single-grade content. Bands are noted here only as reference points for the calibration curve.

| Conceptual band | Grades | Band characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| Lower | 1, 2 | Sentence-level writing, building decoding stamina |
| Lower-Middle | 3, 4 | Paragraph-level writing, building fluency |
| Middle (Garvin baseline) | 5, 6 | Full paragraph craft; Grade 5 is the Garvin baseline |
| Upper | 7, 8 | Abstraction, perspective, and weighing more than one side of an idea |

The Garvin reference examples (from the Grammaropolis Writing Framework) are calibrated to Grade 5. All other grades are derived by scaling up or down from this baseline.

---

## Per-grade calibration targets

### Grade 1

**Reading level**: Early-fluent readers. Building stamina.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1 with 1 Tier 2 reach per worked example. Tier 2 reach must be in context.

**Sentence length**: Average 6-8 words. Maximum 13 words. Simple sentences predominate.

**Sentence structure**: Mostly simple. One compound sentence per worked example acceptable with "and" or "but."

**Worked example length per technique**: 30-55 words.

**Abstraction level**: Concrete with rare simple abstract ("happy," "scared"). No metaphor unless direct ("the sun is like a giant flashlight").

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- Graphical techniques (Cluster, Venn Diagram, 5W & H) stay simple: a small radiating-bubble Cluster with 3-4 connections, a "same/different" two-column layout for the Venn Diagram instead of overlapping circles, and 5W & H simplified to 3W (Who, What, Where).
- Freewrite is 2-4 short sentences with one drift.
- Character Sketch has 4-5 traits.

**CCSS alignment**: W.1.5 (with adult support, focus on a topic and add details to strengthen writing; prewriting is where those details are gathered), plus W.1.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 6-7 years old.

### Grade 2

**Reading level**: Fluent for short passages.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1 with up to 2 Tier 2 reaches per worked example.

**Sentence length**: Average 7-9 words. Maximum 15 words. Some compound sentences.

**Sentence structure**: Mostly simple, with compound sentences in moderation. Rare simple-complex with "because" or "when."

**Worked example length per technique**: 40-65 words.

**Abstraction level**: Concrete predominates. Simple emotional abstractions allowed.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- 5W & H full form (all six slots).
- Venn Diagram introduces overlapping-circles form.
- Character Sketch has 5-6 traits.

**CCSS alignment**: W.2.5 (with adult support, focus on a topic and add details to strengthen writing; prewriting is where those details are gathered), plus W.2.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 7-8 years old. Some self-awareness.

### Grade 3

**Reading level**: Fluent for paragraph-length work.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-2 freely. Up to 3 Tier 2 reaches per worked example.

**Sentence length**: Average 8-10 words. Maximum 18 words. Compound and simple-complex both common.

**Sentence structure**: Full range of simple and compound. Simple-complex with one subordinate clause acceptable.

**Worked example length per technique**: 55-85 words.

**Abstraction level**: Concrete with occasional abstract. Simple metaphors acceptable.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques full-form.
- Cluster has 6-8 bubbles.
- Character Sketch has 6-7 traits.

**CCSS alignment**: W.3.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.3.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 8-9 years old.

### Grade 4

**Reading level**: Skilled readers comfortable with multi-paragraph work.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-2 freely. Up to 3 Tier 2 reaches. Rare Tier 3 with clear context.

**Sentence length**: Average 9-12 words. Maximum 20 words.

**Sentence structure**: Full range. Simple-complex common. Some compound-complex.

**Worked example length per technique**: 70-105 words.

**Abstraction level**: Concrete with comfortable abstract. Metaphor and simile in measured use.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques at near-full depth.
- Cluster has 7-9 bubbles.
- Character Sketch has 7-8 traits.

**CCSS alignment**: W.4.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.4.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 9-10 years old.

### Grade 5 (Garvin baseline)

**Reading level**: Skilled readers. The Garvin reference level.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-2 freely. Occasional Tier 3 with context. Vocabulary in worked examples matches the Garvin reference verbatim where possible.

**Sentence length**: Average 9-13 words. Maximum 22 words.

**Sentence structure**: Full range. Compound, complex, compound-complex all in play.

**Worked example length per technique**: 100-160 words. Matches the Garvin reference examples.

**Abstraction level**: Concrete and abstract both freely available.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques at full depth.
- This is the calibration baseline; all other grades are derived by scaling up or down.
- Garvin source story is calibrated for this grade by default.

**CCSS alignment**: W.5.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.5.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 10-11 years old. Self-aware, capable of low-key humor.

### Grade 6 (the CCSS pedagogical break begins)

**Reading level**: Advanced for the elementary range.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-2-3 freely. Tier 3 expected for academic content.

**Sentence length**: Average 10-14 words. Maximum 24 words.

**Sentence structure**: Full range. Multi-clause sentences acceptable.

**Worked example length per technique**: 110-170 words.

**Abstraction level**: Abstraction handles freely. Argument-quality reasoning begins.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques at full depth.
- The framing shift from "opinion" (G5) to "argument" (G6+) begins to show in worked examples.
- Cluster may include sub-clusters (multi-level radiating).
- Character Sketch may include a contradiction.

**CCSS alignment**: W.6.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.6.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 11-12 years old. Observational.

### Grade 7

**Reading level**: Advanced readers comfortable with abstract argument.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-3 freely.

**Sentence length**: Average 11-16 words. Maximum 26 words. Complex sentences common.

**Sentence structure**: Full range. Parenthetical insertions, embedded clauses, gerund phrases all available.

**Worked example length per technique**: 120-200 words.

**Abstraction level**: Abstract concepts handle freely. Figurative language expected in some techniques (Freewrite, Character Sketch). Seventh-grade craft moves (formal sourcing, complex character development) become available.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques at full depth, with sophistication elevation.
- 5W & H may include H questions that require analysis, not just fact-gathering.
- Venn Diagram may use more nuanced overlap criteria.
- Character Sketch may include explicit internal contradiction.

**CCSS alignment**: W.7.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.7.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 12-13 years old. Capable of subtle humor and self-awareness.

### Grade 8

**Reading level**: Highest in the Grades 1-8 range.

**Vocabulary**: Tier 1-3 freely. Tier 3 expected in worked examples that demonstrate explanatory or persuasive purposes.

**Sentence length**: Average 12-17 words. Maximum 28 words.

**Sentence structure**: Full range. Multi-clause sentences common.

**Worked example length per technique**: 130-220 words.

**Abstraction level**: Abstract concepts and figurative language both expected. The full seventh-grade craft moves are available, with Grade 8 elevation in sophistication.

**Pedagogical adjustments**:
- All 8 techniques at full depth and elevated sophistication.
- Cluster may include multi-level sub-clusters.
- Character Sketch may include multiple contradictions and internal complexity.

**CCSS alignment**: W.8.5 (develop and strengthen writing by planning; prewriting is the planning step), plus W.8.3 when the source story is a narrative, as the Garvin story is. Cite no other writing standard for a prewriting set.

**Tone**: The narrator sounds 13-14 years old. Aware of audience and authorial stance.

## Per-grade cross-check questions

When generating, the skill should be able to answer "yes" to all of these for the chosen grade:

**Grade 1 cross-check:**
- Are average sentence lengths under 9 words?
- Are Tier 2 reaches limited to 1 per worked example with clear context?
- Is the narrator sounding 6-7 years old?

**Grade 2 cross-check:**
- Are average sentence lengths under 10 words?
- Are Tier 2 reaches limited to 2 per worked example?
- Does the output have at least one full Q&A structure (for 5W&H) or list (for Brainstorm)?

**Grade 3 cross-check:**
- Are average sentence lengths under 11 words?
- Are grade-level linking words ("because," "therefore," "since," "for example") present where appropriate?
- Is the narrator sounding 8-9 years old?

**Grade 4 cross-check:**
- Are average sentence lengths under 13 words?
- Are grade-level linking words ("for instance," "in order to," "in addition") present where appropriate?
- Are reasons supported by "facts and details," not just opinion?

**Grade 5 cross-check (Garvin baseline):**
- Does the prose match the Garvin reference level for vocabulary and sentence variety?
- Are ideas in a logical order?
- Could a Grade 5 reader understand without external help?

**Grade 6 cross-check:**
- Does the framing begin to use "claim" rather than "opinion" where appropriate?
- Is a more formal style emerging?
- Is the narrator sounding 11-12 years old?

**Grade 7 cross-check:**
- Are counterclaims acknowledged where the worked example involves the persuasive purpose?
- Is sourcing convention emerging (name + credential + verb + quote)?
- Does the prose carry developmental sophistication appropriate to 12-13?

**Grade 8 cross-check:**
- Are counterclaims explicitly distinguished from the writer's own claim?
- Is formal style maintained throughout?
- Are figurative language, abstraction, and complex sentences in active use?

## Vocabulary tier definitions (working reference)

Tier definitions from research consensus on grade-appropriate vocabulary:

- **Tier 1**: Everyday conversational words. "Run, jump, eat, hot, cold, mom, friend." Acquired through ordinary exposure. Early-grade reading material is mostly Tier 1.
- **Tier 2**: High-frequency academic words across content areas. "Examine, predict, evidence, structure, consequence." Learned through schooling and reading. Increasingly common from Grade 3 upward.
- **Tier 3**: Domain-specific or low-frequency words. "Photosynthesis, escalation, predilection." Learned through direct instruction in a specific subject. Appears in Grade 5+ but should be supported with context.

When in doubt, default to the tier table above for the chosen grade.

## When the skill is calibration-uncertain

If the skill can't confidently calibrate to the requested grade (e.g., the source story includes inherently complex content that doesn't regrade well to the lower grades), it should:

1. Generate worked examples at the closest grade it can hit confidently
2. Flag the calibration issue in a note to the teacher
3. Suggest either choosing a different source story or accepting a one-grade shift

Do not silently miscalibrate. A Grade 1 worked example with Tier 3 vocabulary is worse than no worked example at all.

## Skill input parameter

The skill's `grade` parameter accepts one of: `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, `5`, `6`, `7`, `8`. Grammaropolis's catalog doesn't include Kindergarten; decline a Kindergarten request rather than accepting `K`. Single grade only. If a teacher wants to compare grades, run the skill once per grade.
